# Portable cluster v0: system spec and schematics (2026-10-26)

Martin, 26 October: "we need to start building schematics for portable cluster systems for local AI, I think that's
the play." This is the first schematic set for the four-node portable cluster (the Cell Zero brief, made concrete).
System-level schematics: what connects to what, with what power, data and air. The board-level schematic of the power
distribution board is Martin's, in KiCad, from the requirements in section 3.

Every number is labelled: **measured** (a Spliced note), **sourced** (a published spec, linked), or **estimate**
(to be replaced by a measurement before it is built on).

Drawings: `block.svg` (the system), `power.svg` (the power tree), `network.svg` (data), `layout.svg` (top view and
airflow). Generated by `draw.py` from the numbers in it, so a changed number redraws every sheet.

## 1. The system

Four compute nodes, each a Strix Halo Mini-ITX board (AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395, 128 GB), in one case with one mains
inlet, one network port, front-to-back air. Serves a frontier-class open model to the local network, offline:
DeepSeek V4 Flash on one or two nodes, GLM-5.2 on three (`fabric/moe.py`), or four smaller models side by side.

| node fact | value | label |
|---|---|---|
| board | Mini-ITX, 170 x 170 mm | sourced (Mini-ITX format) |
| power connectors | 24-pin ATX, plus a CPU connector (8-pin or 12-pin: sources disagree) | sourced, to verify |
| power | 120 W sustained, 140 W boost (Framework setting) | sourced |
| peak at the wall, one mini PC | 160 to 180 W | sourced (ServeTheHome) |
| idle | about 20 W package | measured (Fabric cost table) |
| network | 5 Gbit Ethernet, 2 x USB4, PCIe x4 slot | sourced (Framework, reviews) |
| cooling | 120 mm fan on a six-heatpipe heatsink | sourced |
| width with cooler | at most 96.8 mm (Framework's own case is that wide) | sourced, upper bound |

## 2. Power budget

| load | each | count | total | label |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| node, peak at 12 V | 165 W | 4 | 660 W | estimate (180 W wall less supply loss) |
| network switch | 20 W | 1 | 20 W | estimate |
| intake fans, 120 mm | 3 W | 4 | 12 W | estimate |
| controller and front panel | 3 W | 1 | 3 W | estimate |
| **total at 12 V** | | | **695 W (58 A)** | |

So one 12 V supply of 1,000 W gives about 30% headroom, and the wall draw at full load is about 770 W (estimate, at
about 90% supply efficiency): within one 10 A mains socket at 230 V, and within a 15 A circuit at 120 V.

## 3. The power distribution board (Martin's KiCad design)

Requirements:
- Input: 12 V, up to 60 A, from the supply through a busbar or 8 AWG leads.
- Four node branches, each: an electronic fuse or hot-swap controller at about 20 A, a current and voltage monitor on
  I2C, an enable line from the controller. Each branch feeds the node's CPU connector directly and a 12 V-to-ATX
  converter (picoPSU class) for the 24-pin connector.
- Branches for the switch (12 V) and the fans (12 V, PWM from the controller).
- A 5 V and 3.3 V buck for the controller.
- Sequencing: nodes start one at a time, 2 s apart, so their inrush does not add up.
- Branch wiring: 16 AWG at 14 A (estimate; check against the chosen connector's rating).

## 4. Network

Each node's 5 Gbit Ethernet to a small unmanaged switch inside the case; one port of the switch to the outside world.
Fabric's plan puts the network under 1% of the time per token when a model is split in a pipeline, even at 10 Gbit,
so 5 Gbit is enough for v0. USB4 between neighbouring nodes is a later option for expert-parallel splits.

## 5. Air

About 700 W of heat. Air needed for a 15 C rise, from the heat equation (Q = m c dT): about 0.039 m3/s, or 82 CFM
(estimate, sea-level air). Four 120 mm intake fans in front, one per node lane, each moving about 20 CFM through the
lane at low speed, with each node's own cooler fan doing the rest. To verify with a fan-and-thermometer mock-up.

## 6. Size (estimate, to confirm with the board drawing)

Nodes on edge in four lanes of 97 mm: about 388 mm wide. Depth: 30 mm intake and grille, 170 mm board, 120 mm rear bay
for the supply, the switch and the distribution board: about 320 mm. Height: about 220 mm. About 27 litres, a little
smaller than a cabin suitcase.
